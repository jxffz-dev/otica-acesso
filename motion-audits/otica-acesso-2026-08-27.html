Overall

Unusually disciplined for what it is — reduced-motion is respected everywhere motion exists, and nothing fires on a high-frequency or keyboard action. The one real gap sits exactly where it hurts most: the scheduler's button swaps its entire content with zero transition, on the site's single conversion action. Everything else is craft-level, not urgency-level.

CriticalEmil

The scheduler's button swaps its entire content with no transition

WhatOnce both store and shift are picked, the button's children switch in a single render from a plain <span>Escolha a loja e o turno</span> to the real WhatsApp link (icon + "Confirmar pelo WhatsApp" + arrow). No AnimatePresence, no CSS transition — the button just changes.

Why it mattersThis is the one interaction in the entire site that is the product — every other page exists to get someone here. A user who just made two small decisions gets zero acknowledgment that anything happened; the only feedback is that the text is suddenly different.

Recommended motionEmil's territory, not Jakub's or Jhey's — a utility action, so no bounce, no spring. A CSS-transition-based crossfade between the two label states (not keyframes, so it stays interruptible if the selection changes mid-animation), ~160ms, ease-out.

Issues to address
ImportantJakub

One entrance animation, reused 11 times on a single page

WhatFadeIn (opacity + 16px translateY, 500ms) wraps Hero, TrustBar, Categories, StylesShowcase, SocialProof, ExamCta and Stores on the homepage — 11 instances once staggered children are counted — and reappears 2-4 more times on every other page. It's the site's only entrance pattern, with no distinction between a hero moment and a routine trust bar.

Why it mattersUsed once or twice, this exact recipe is good, restrained polish. Used 11 times in one scroll, it stops reading as intentional and starts reading as a template default — every section announces itself the same way, so nothing does.

Recommended motionKeep the full treatment for one or two real moments — the hero, maybe the exam CTA — and let routine sections (trust bar, store cards) appear with little or no motion. Hierarchy of motion should mirror hierarchy of content.

ImportantJakub

Two different press-feedback languages for the same kind of button

WhatThe shared Button component's only press feedback is a 1px translate-y. But 9 primary CTAs on the homepage — and only the homepage — stack an extra active:scale-[0.97] (or 0.98 on cards) on top of that. Every button on every other page gets the 1px-only treatment.

Why it mattersThe header's "Agendar exame" button appears on every page with identical styling — but it only gets the extra scale feedback when it happens to render on the homepage. Users won't articulate this, but the site's most-repeated action feels subtly different depending on where they click it.

Recommended motionMove the scale feedback into the shared Button component itself (or drop it from the homepage instances) so every button in the same visual family presses the same way, everywhere.

Which lens carried this audit

Referenced most

Emil Kowalski — Restraint & Speed

This is a low-frequency utility site for a brand that must never feel gimmicky — Emil's frequency-gate is the right default lens for almost everything here, and the one real gap sits squarely in his territory: a utility action that changed state with no feedback at all. Jakub's lens caught the two consistency gaps; Jhey's marked the one place — and only one — worth spending delight.

  • Lean EmilRequire a stated reason before any new element gets an entrance animation by default.
  • Lean JakubAudit every button and card for its press/hover feedback and unify them into one shared recipe in the Button component, instead of per-instance overrides.
  • Lean JheySpend a real one-shot moment on the scheduler's ready state — a small icon settle or a soft highlight sweep — since it's the only true "you did it" beat in the whole flow.
